Process of Fertilisation

  1. Male and female gametes are produced.
  2. The male gamete reaches the female gamete.
  3. The two gametes fuse.
  4. A zygote is formed.
  5. The zygote begins to divide and develop.

Types of Fertilisation

1. External Fertilisation

External fertilisation occurs when the fusion of male and female gametes takes place outside the body of the female.

Example: Many fish and amphibians.

2. Internal Fertilisation

Internal fertilisation occurs when the fusion of male and female gametes takes place inside the body of the female.

Example: Humans, birds and many mammals.

Pollination

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower.

Self-Pollination

Self-pollination occurs when pollen is transferred to the stigma of the same flower or another flower on the same plant.

Cross-Pollination

Cross-pollination occurs when pollen is transferred from a flower of one plant to a flower of another plant of the same species.

Reproduction in Flowering Plants

The flower is the reproductive structure of a flowering plant. It contains male and female reproductive parts.

Male Reproductive Part - Stamen

The stamen is the male reproductive part of a flower.

  • Anther - produces pollen grains.
  • Filament - supports the anther.

Female Reproductive Part - Pistil or Carpel

The pistil or carpel is the female reproductive part of a flower.

  • Stigma - receives pollen grains.
  • Style - connects the stigma with the ovary.
  • Ovary - contains ovules.
  • Ovule - contains the female reproductive cell.

Fertilisation in Flowering Plants

After pollination, a pollen grain germinates on the stigma and forms a pollen tube. The pollen tube grows towards the ovule. The male reproductive cell reaches the female reproductive cell and their fusion forms a zygote.

After fertilisation, the zygote develops into an embryo, the ovule develops into a seed and the ovary generally develops into a fruit.

Male Reproductive System

The male reproductive system is a group of organs that produces male gametes called sperm and helps in their transport.

Main Parts of the Male Reproductive System

  • Testes
  • Scrotum
  • Vas deferens
  • Seminal vesicles
  • Prostate gland
  • Urethra
  • Penis

Testes

The testes produce sperm and the male sex hormone testosterone.

Scrotum

The scrotum is a pouch that contains the testes. It helps maintain a temperature suitable for sperm formation.

Vas Deferens

The vas deferens are tubes that transport sperm from the testes towards the urethra.

Seminal Vesicles and Prostate Gland

These glands add fluids to the sperm. The mixture of sperm and these fluids forms semen.

Urethra

The urethra is a tube through which semen passes out of the male reproductive system.

Sperm - Main Parts

  • Head - contains the nucleus and genetic material.
  • Middle region - contains many mitochondria.
  • Tail - helps the sperm move.

Female Reproductive System

The female reproductive system produces female gametes and provides the organs required for fertilisation and development of the embryo.

Main Parts of the Female Reproductive System

  • Ovaries
  • Oviducts or Fallopian tubes
  • Uterus
  • Cervix
  • Vagina

Ovaries

The ovaries produce and release eggs. They also produce important female reproductive hormones.

Oviducts or Fallopian Tubes

The oviducts are tubes that connect the ovaries to the uterus. Fertilisation usually occurs in an oviduct.

Uterus

The uterus is a muscular organ where the embryo implants and develops during pregnancy.

Cervix

The cervix is the lower narrow part of the uterus that connects the uterus with the vagina.

Vagina

The vagina is a muscular canal connecting the cervix to the outside of the body.

Female Reproductive Process

  1. An egg develops in an ovary.
  2. The egg is released during ovulation.
  3. The egg enters the oviduct.
  4. If a sperm fuses with the egg, fertilisation occurs.
  5. The resulting zygote begins to divide.
  6. The developing embryo reaches the uterus.
  7. The embryo implants in the uterine lining and continues to develop.

Important Human Reproduction Sequence

Testes → Sperm formation → Sperm transport

Ovary → Egg formation → Ovulation

Sperm + Egg → Fertilisation → Zygote

Zygote → Embryo → Foetus → Birth
